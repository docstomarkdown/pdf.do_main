A PDF that is too big to email or upload is usually big for one reason: pictures. Text takes very little room. A scanned page or a photo can take hundreds of times more. So the first job is to find out what is taking the space, and the second is to pick the gentlest way to shrink it.

## Find out what is taking the space

Open [PDF size analyser](/pdf-size-analyzer) and choose your file. It shows a bar and a list: pictures, fonts, page content, and the rest. It only reads the file, and nothing leaves your device.

If pictures are most of the file, shrinking the pictures is where the gain is. If they are only a small share, there is less to win.

## Try the gentle way first

[Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) has two modes. **Smart** rewrites the file more efficiently and recompresses images that can be recompressed. Text stays selectable and the pages look the same.

Smart will do almost nothing for some files, and that is not a fault. Scans and phone photos are usually stored as JPEG pictures, which are already compressed, and Smart cannot compress them further.

## When you need an exact size

**Target size** keeps lowering picture quality until the file fits the size you choose. Pages are turned into images when needed, so the text is no longer selectable. The tool tells you when it did that and shows the settings it used. Look at the result before you send it.

| Your situation | What to try |
|---|---|
| A report made on a computer, mostly text | Smart mode |
| A scan or a stack of photos | Target size, or fewer and smaller photos before you make the PDF |
| One or two pages you do not need | [Delete PDF pages](/delete-pdf-pages) first |
| A limit such as 5 MB per email | [Split PDF by size](/split-pdf-by-size) into parts that each fit |

## Check the result

Open the smaller file and look at a few pages at full size. Check that the text you need is still readable, and that stamps, signatures and small print survived. Keep your original: the tools save a new file and never change yours.
